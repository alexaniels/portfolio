An Interactive Phishing Awareness Scenario

A branching, code-built scenario teaching IBA students to recognize phishing

Overview

A solo project built from scratch in HTML, SCSS, and JavaScript. Instead of telling students what phishing looks like, the scenario puts them inside a realistic phishing attempt and lets their choices play out. I handled everything from interaction logic to visual design and front-end build.

Role
Designer

Phishing awareness,

Tools used

Challenge

IBA students are easy phishing targets: they’re online constantly and receive a steady stream of institutional email. Abstract warnings don’t build the skill of spotting a wrong sender address, false urgency, or a suspicious link. I wanted the lesson to come from experience, not a warning label.

Concept

The scenario opens with a spoofed email from “IBA IT” (iba.@iba.clk) and not (iba@iba.dk), warning of a security breach and pushing the student to reset their password. It then branches three ways: change password leads to a fake login page and a hacked reveal; ignore it leaves a lingering risk; delete it prompts the student to block and report the sender. Each path ends with a short security lesson tied to the choice just made.

Process

Design started in Figma, mapping out the full branching logic as a flowchart before any code was written — tracing every possible path a student could take through the scenario and what each one needed to teach. That flowchart became the blueprint for the JavaScript state logic later on. From there the build moved into HTML for structure, SCSS for styling, and JavaScript for the interaction and branching logic, with the phishing email, fake login screen, and “hacked” reveal all designed as in-scenario assets rather than generic stock imagery.

Outcome

The result is a working, publicly hosted interactive scenario that turns a phishing warning into something a student actually experiences and makes choices inside of, with the payoff: real, specific security guidance, arriving at exactly the moment it’s most relevant to what they just chose. Two limitations remain: button placement on mobile, and the scenario has not yet been validated with real users. Next steps are fixing the layout structure and testing with IBA students.
